Overview

Where does performance slip in a process plant?

In energy and chemicals, margin is won or lost in the details: unplanned downtime, slow turnarounds, maintenance backlogs, manual reporting and procedures that drift from how work is really done. Chmltech is led by a process engineer, so we understand unit operations, management of change and process safety as well as Lean and Six Sigma. We map how work actually flows, measure it against clear KPIs and work alongside your operators, engineers and supervisors to remove waste. The result is a culture of continuous improvement that keeps paying dividends long after the project ends.

Signs it's time to act

  • Turnarounds regularly run over schedule or budget
  • The maintenance backlog keeps growing despite overtime
  • MOC and permit paperwork slows routine work to a crawl
  • Shift reports and KPIs are compiled by hand in spreadsheets
  • Operators and management see different numbers for the same unit

Industry challenges

What we see in energy & chemicals.

Unplanned downtime

Reactive maintenance and poor work planning take units offline and erode throughput. Without reliable data it is hard to see which failures matter most.

Turnaround and shutdown overruns

Scope creep, late materials and weak sequencing stretch turnarounds and push costs well past plan.

Process safety and compliance load

PSM, MOC and regulatory reporting are essential, but manual, paper-heavy workflows consume engineering time and invite errors.

Disconnected performance measures

Frontline teams, engineering and executives often track different metrics, so improvement efforts pull in different directions.

How we help

Typical scenarios

What improvement looks like.

Illustrative examples of the kind of change we work on.

Planned shutdown of a process unit

Before: Scope was finalized late and contractors waited on materials, stretching the shutdown past plan.

After: Scope freeze dates, staged materials and a sequenced schedule keep the work on a tighter, more predictable timeline.

Management of change paperwork

Before: Routine MOCs circulated on paper for weeks, waiting for signatures from busy engineers.

After: A risk-tiered digital MOC workflow routes simple changes quickly and reserves full review for higher-risk items.

Daily production reporting

Before: A shift supervisor spent the first hour of each shift copying historian data into a spreadsheet.

After: An automated daily report and a short huddle board free that time for walking the unit and solving problems.

Questions

Frequently asked

Will process improvement compromise process safety or MOC?

No. Safety and PSM requirements are the foundation of every change we propose. Where we streamline MOC or permit workflows, we keep the required reviews and make the low-risk path faster rather than removing controls.

Can you help before or during a turnaround?

Yes. We can review scope, planning and sequencing ahead of a turnaround, and help you capture lessons learned afterwards so the next one runs better.

Do you work with smaller operators and chemical producers?

Yes. Much of our work is with Canadian SMEs, including specialty chemical producers, energy service companies and smaller facilities that need practical improvement without a large consulting team.

How do you measure success?

We agree KPIs and a baseline at the start, covering service levels, productivity, speed and quality. Progress is tracked against those measures and an agreed ROI target throughout the engagement.
