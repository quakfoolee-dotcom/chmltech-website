Overview

What is Business Process Optimization?

BPO is part of the discipline of Business Process Management (BPM). Optimized processes lead to optimized business goals. Your organization faces constant competition, disruptive technologies and changing norms. Optimizing how work actually flows, end to end and across departments, helps you stay ahead of that change instead of reacting to it.

We apply continuous improvement methods including Lean, Six Sigma and other analytical tools, document processes using industry-standard notation, and combine them with change management. Too often, processes are redesigned only to be partially adopted or never implemented. Integrating the human side is what turns a better design into sustained performance.

Signs you need this

  • Work waits in inboxes for approvals or hand-offs between departments
  • The same data is typed into several systems or spreadsheets
  • Errors, rework and exceptions consume a large share of the team's time
  • Results depend on who is working that day, not on a defined process
  • New systems were introduced, but the underlying process never changed

Services

What we deliver

Current-state process maps

We characterize how work really happens today, including information flows, roles, systems and decision points.

Gap and opportunity analysis

We identify waste, bottlenecks, control gaps and opportunities to improve quality, safety, throughput and cost.

Future-state (to-be) design

Redesigned processes that are simple, clear and aligned with your strategic goals, with roles and controls defined.

Automation where it fits

Once unnecessary steps are removed, we automate the revised process in its new form, not the old one.

Change management

Stakeholder engagement, communication and training that secure buy-in, so new processes are adopted and sustained.

Monitoring and KPIs

Measures and routines to look at performance at micro and macro levels and fine-tune until you get the desired results.

Real examples

How optimization increases efficiency.

Purchase orders

Before: Multiple purchase orders clogged the purchasing queue because approvals happened by email.

After: A dedicated workflow system now lets employees choose the item and send it for approval automatically.

Travel reimbursement

Before: A paper-based process meant finance misread numbers and reimbursed the wrong amounts.

After: Employees now enter amounts in a digital form, which removes the misreading errors.

Employee onboarding

Before: HR manually emailed IT after each signed agreement to provision laptops and access.

After: An automatic trigger now notifies IT as soon as the employee signs the agreement.

Questions

Frequently asked

How is BPO different from process improvement?

Process improvement often targets a specific area or problem. BPO looks at end-to-end processes across departments, such as order-to-cash or procure-to-pay, and aligns them with strategy, systems and controls.

Do we need new software?

Not necessarily. We optimize the process first. Sometimes existing systems are enough once the process is fixed; where automation helps, we automate the improved process, not the old one.

How do you make changes stick?

We involve the people who do the work, define clear roles and measures, and build change management and monitoring into the project so teams don't revert to old habits.

Which processes should we start with?

Usually a process that is visibly painful, frequent and important to customers or cash flow. We help you choose using value, effort and risk.
