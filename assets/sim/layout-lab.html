How the model works
  • Both layouts hold about the same racks (780 and 840 pallet positions a level, 780 SKUs) in the same 72 × 52 m building, with five dock doors each.
  • Orders arrive through the day (30 waiting at 07:00, the rest by 14:00), about 3 lines each. Pickers walk at 0.9 m/s with a cart, take 20 s a case pick, 45 s to start an order and 30 s to drop it at packing.
  • A receiving-heavy day: at 300 orders a day, 14 inbound trucks (5 full loads of 24 pallets, the rest part loads), far more pallets in than go out. Each unloads at 2.5 minutes a pallet into staging. One forklift (two from 400 orders a day) puts each pallet away above its SKU's pick face, at 2 m/s.
  • Every 20 packed orders make an outbound pallet, moved to shipping staging by a pallet jack. Carriers collect at 10:30, 12:30 and 14:30.
  • People and vehicles move on a 1 m grid. When two meet within a metre, one gives way and squeezes past after a few seconds.
  • Labour at $40 an hour loaded, 250 days a year; truck detention at $65 an hour after two hours on site. The table is the full simulated shift; the animation replays it 12 times faster than life (with reduced motion turned on, it shows the floor at 10:30, still). Trucks still on site at 15:00 are counted up to 15:00.
