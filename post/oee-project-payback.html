Does the OEE Project Pay Back? Testing a $1M Investment

Quak Foo LeeNov 11, 20267 min read

An automation proposal promises higher OEE. Here is how to turn that promise into a payback period, and how to see which assumptions carry the case.

Post 1 measured a packaging line at 73.4% OEE. Post 2 showed that the lost capacity is worth about $1.08M of revenue a year on a $3.0M line. Now a supplier proposes a $1M automation cell for that line. Should the owner sign?

Step 1: state the gain in OEE terms

The proposal should say which factors improve and by how much. In this case, automated loading and faster changeovers lift availability and speed, and in-line inspection lifts quality.

Table 5. OEE before and after the project

FactorTodayTargetChange
Availability85%90%+5 points
Performance90%95%+5 points
Quality96%97%+1 point
OEE73.4%82.9%+9.5 points

Moving from 73.4% to 82.9% means 12.9% more good output from the same scheduled hours. On a $3.0M line, that is about $388,000 of extra revenue a year, provided the output can be sold.

Step 2: convert revenue to profit, then add the other savings

Extra units need material, so only the contribution margin reaches the bottom line. At a 50% margin, $388,000 of revenue becomes $194,000 of profit. The project also changes labour and running costs.

Table 6. Annual benefit at full performance

ItemBasisPer year
Profit on extra output$387,868 revenue × 50% contribution margin$193,934
Labour savedOne operator position per shift on two shifts at $60,000, less a quarter of an operator per shift to tend the cell$90,000
Indirect savingsLower hiring, training and turnover cost$30,000
Maintenance and energyService contract plus 2 kW over 4,000 hours−$10,800
Net annual benefit$303,134

New equipment rarely reaches its target in the first month. This case assumes the first year delivers half of the OEE gain, so year 1 brings $206,167 and every later year brings $303,134.

Step 3: read the result three ways

  • Payback: 3.6 years. The cumulative benefit passes the $1M investment during year 4.
  • Net present value: about $774,000. This is the ten-year benefit discounted at 10% a year, less the investment. A positive value means the project earns more than a 10% return.
  • Financed cash flow: positive from year 1. A ten-year loan at 10% costs about $158,600 a year. The project covers that payment and leaves about $47,600 in year 1 and $144,600 in each later year.

Step 4: test the assumptions

Two inputs carry most of the risk: the contribution margin and whether the promised OEE gain arrives. Table 7 shows the payback when each one moves.

Table 7. Payback period and ten-year NPV under different assumptions

Contribution marginFull OEE gainNPVHalf the OEE gainNPV
30%4.7 years$333K6.2 years−$4K
50% (base case)3.6 years$774K5.1 years$213K
70%3.0 years$1.22M4.4 years$430K
100% (revenue counted as profit)2.4 years$1.88M3.7 years$755K

The last row is the mistake to avoid. Counting all extra revenue as profit makes the project look as if it pays back in 2.4 years. With a realistic margin it takes 3.6 years, and if only half of the OEE gain arrives it takes more than 5.

Before you sign

  1. Measure the baseline first. Record OEE and the six losses for at least four weeks. A supplier's gain is only as credible as the starting number.
  2. Buy the cheap points before the expensive ones. Changeover practice, minor-stop logging and preventive maintenance often recover several points for little capital. Those gains also shrink what the big project has left to deliver, so run the case on the improved baseline.
  3. Confirm the sales. If the market will not take 13% more product, replace the revenue line with the overtime or shift cost you would actually save.
  4. Ask for the gain in writing. Have the supplier state the expected availability, speed and quality figures, and tie part of the payment to an acceptance test.
  5. Plan the ramp-up. Training, spare parts and a named owner for the cell decide whether year 1 delivers half the gain or none of it.

Takeaway. A $1M investment on a $3M line can pay back in under four years and cover its own loan payments, but only at a realistic margin and only if the OEE gain is delivered. Run the case with your own numbers before the supplier runs it for you.
