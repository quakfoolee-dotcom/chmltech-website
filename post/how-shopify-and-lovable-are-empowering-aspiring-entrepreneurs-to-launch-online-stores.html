Building a Shopify Store with Lovable: What the AI Integration Does, Costs and Suits

Since late 2025, Lovable, an AI app builder, has offered an official integration with Shopify that lets you build an online store by describing it in plain language. This article was first published in October 2025, shortly after the launch. It has been rewritten to explain what the integration actually does and does not do, what it costs, and when it makes sense for a new or small business. The earlier version included statistics we could not trace to a source; those have been removed.

Who does what

PartRoleCost
ShopifyThe commerce engine: products, inventory, checkout, payments, taxes, shipping, orders and a large app ecosystemMonthly plan plus payment processing fees
LovableAn AI app builder: you describe the storefront in plain language and it generates the site and its codeFree tier with daily credits; paid plans from about $25 a month, with credits used per change
YouThe products, brand, prices, photos, policies and customer serviceYour time, stock and marketing

The important point is the division of labour. Lovable builds the storefront, the pages customers see. Shopify still runs the business underneath: checkout, payments, taxes, orders and inventory. Lovable does not analyse your customers or make sales recommendations on its own; that comes from Shopify's tools and apps, or from your own analysis.

How it works

  1. Describe the store in a Lovable project, for example "a store for handmade candles with a calm, natural look and three featured products".
  2. Connect Shopify. Lovable can set up a new test (sandbox) store or connect to an existing Shopify store.
  3. Add and edit products, including AI-drafted descriptions you should check and rewrite in your own voice.
  4. Refine by chatting: change layouts, colours, sections and copy by describing what you want.
  5. Claim and publish the store in Shopify, choose a plan, set up payments, shipping and taxes, and go live.

What it costs

Shopify's plans for a full online store start at its Basic plan, which in 2026 costs about $39 a month in the United States when billed monthly, with a discount for annual billing; Grow and Advanced cost more, and each sale also carries a card processing fee that falls as you move up plans. Lovable has a free tier with a small number of daily credits, and paid plans from about $25 a month for a monthly allowance of credits, where each request for a change uses credits. Shopify apps, a domain, photography and marketing come on top. Check both vendors' pricing pages in Canadian dollars before you budget; both changed their plans during 2026.

When it makes sense

SituationGood fit?Why
Testing a product idea quicklyYesA presentable store in hours, not weeks, so you can test demand cheaply
A custom look beyond standard themesYes, with careThe AI can produce distinctive designs, but you own maintaining the code
A small catalogue, simple shippingYesFew products and settings to configure
An existing store that works wellRarelyA standard Shopify theme with good content is easier to maintain
Complex B2B pricing, many variants, integrationsNot aloneNeeds Shopify configuration and apps, and often a developer

Things to watch

  • Maintenance. A storefront generated as custom code is yours to keep working as Shopify and browsers change. A standard theme gets updates from its developer.
  • Credits add up. Many small change requests use credits quickly; plan your changes and batch them.
  • Content still matters most. AI-generated product text is generic. Your own descriptions, photos and policies are what convert visitors and rank in search; see growing a small online store.
  • Check the basics before launch: mobile speed, checkout, shipping rates, taxes, returns policy and privacy policy.

Worked example: testing a product idea

A maker wants to know whether a range of handmade cutting boards will sell online before investing in stock. Using the integration, she builds a store in an afternoon with five products, publishes it on a Shopify Basic plan, and spends a modest amount on social ads over a month. Her costs for the test are the Shopify plan, a Lovable paid plan for a month, a domain and the ads. At the end of the month she has real data: visits, add-to-cart rate, orders and questions from customers. That evidence, not the store itself, is what the tool buys her. The approach is illustrative; the point is to treat an AI-built store as a fast, cheap experiment.

A launch checklist

AreaCheck before going live
ProductsEvery product has your own description, at least three clear photos, price, weight and stock
CheckoutPlace a test order end to end, including a refund
PaymentsPayment provider set up and payouts going to the right bank account
ShippingRates for each zone you ship to; free-shipping threshold if you offer one
TaxesGST/HST and provincial sales tax settings checked with your accountant
PoliciesReturns, shipping, privacy and terms pages published and linked in the footer
MobileHome, product and checkout pages tested on a phone, on mobile data
SearchPage titles and descriptions written; store connected to Google Search Console
MeasurementAnalytics working; UTM links on every social post and ad

Alternatives worth comparing

The Lovable route is one of several ways to get a Shopify store online. A standard Shopify theme, free or paid, is still the most common choice, and it is the easiest to maintain because the theme developer handles updates. Shopify's own editor and built-in AI writing tools can draft product text without a separate subscription. Website builders such as Wix and Squarespace include e-commerce in their plans and suit businesses whose site is mainly about services with a small shop on the side. For a business that already has a custom website, a Shopify "Buy Button" can add checkout to existing pages. The right choice depends less on the tool than on who will maintain the store a year from now.

Our view

The integration is a genuine shortcut for getting a presentable store in front of customers quickly, and that has real value for testing ideas. It does not remove the work that makes a store succeed: a product people want, honest and useful product pages, fair shipping, and steady marketing. Use it to start fast, and plan from day one how the store will be maintained once it is earning money.
