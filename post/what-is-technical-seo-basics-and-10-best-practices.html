Technical SEO in 2026: Basics and 10 Best Practices for Small Business Websites

Quak Foo LeeMar 29, 2023Updated Nov 13, 20266 min read

Technical SEO is the work that lets search engines find, read and index your pages. Content and links decide how well a page ranks; technical SEO decides whether it can rank at all. This guide was first written in 2023 and has been rewritten for 2026. It also absorbs an older general SEO guide from this site, so the basics of how Google Search works are covered here too.

How a page gets into Google

Google finds a page through links or a sitemap, crawls it, renders it as a mobile browser would, decides whether to index it, and then chooses when to show it. A failure at any step stops everything after it. Most technical SEO problems on small business sites are at steps 2 and 4: pages blocked by accident, or several URLs for the same page.

Ten best practices, and how to check each

PracticeWhat to doWhere to check
1. Let Google crawl what mattersCheck robots.txt does not block CSS, JavaScript or key folders; check your CDN or firewall does not block Googlebot.Search Console › URL Inspection › Test live URL
2. Publish an XML sitemapList only canonical, indexable pages; submit it and reference it in robots.txt.Search Console › Sitemaps
3. One URL per pageRedirect http to https and the non-preferred host; set a self-referencing canonical; avoid duplicate parameter URLs.Page indexing report: 'Duplicate' reasons
4. Use HTTPS everywhereValid certificate, no mixed content, 301 redirects from http.Browser address bar; a site crawler
5. Make the mobile page completeGoogle indexes the mobile version, so the same content, headings and structured data must be on it.URL Inspection › View crawled page
6. Meet Core Web VitalsLCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or less, at the 75th percentile of real visits.Search Console › Core Web Vitals; PageSpeed Insights
7. Keep the content in the HTMLIf content appears only after JavaScript runs, check Google renders it, or render on the server.URL Inspection › rendered HTML
8. Build clear internal linksEvery important page within three clicks of the home page, with descriptive link text; no orphan pages.A site crawler; Links report

What changed since 2023

  • INP replaced FID. Interaction to Next Paint became a Core Web Vital on 12 March 2024, replacing First Input Delay. It measures how quickly a page responds to every click, tap and key press during a visit, not only the first. A good score is 200 milliseconds or less. Heavy scripts, chat widgets and tag managers are the usual cause of poor INP.
  • Mobile-first indexing is complete. Google crawls and indexes with its smartphone crawler, so a stripped-down mobile page means a stripped-down index entry.
  • AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same index. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in them. To be eligible as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Google also says you do not need an llms.txt file or special markup for its AI features.
  • Google-Extended is separate. This robots.txt token controls whether your content is used to train and ground Google's Gemini models. Blocking it does not remove you from Search or AI Overviews; robots.txt rules for Googlebot and the nosnippet and noindex controls are what govern Search. Other AI companies run their own crawlers, such as GPTBot and ClaudeBot, each with its own robots.txt token. Decide on these as a content licensing choice, not an SEO one.

The basics that still decide rankings

Technical SEO removes obstacles. Rankings still come from pages that answer a searcher's question better than the alternatives, written by people who know the subject, on a site others link to and mention. For a small business that means one clear page per service, a page for each location you serve, a complete Google Business Profile, and articles that show real experience: worked examples, photos of your own work, and numbers from your own projects.

A one-hour monthly check

  1. Search Console › Page indexing: look for new "Not indexed" reasons on pages that should be indexed.
  2. Search Console › Core Web Vitals: look for URLs that moved to "Poor" or "Needs improvement".
  3. Search Console › Performance: compare clicks and impressions with last month for your top 20 pages.
  4. Run a crawler over the site for broken links, redirect chains and missing titles.
  5. Inspect one key page with URL Inspection to confirm Google sees the content you expect.
