ERP vs MRP: Which Does Your Manufacturing Business Need?

Quak Foo LeeMar 19, 2023Updated Nov 13, 20265 min read

MRP (material requirements planning) and ERP (enterprise resource planning) are often confused because every manufacturing ERP contains MRP. The practical question for a small or mid-sized manufacturer is whether a focused MRP system is enough, or whether it is time for an ERP. This article, first published in 2023, has been rewritten as a decision guide.

How they relate

Side by side

MRP systemERP system
Main purposePlan materials and productionRun the whole business on one system
Typical usersPlanners, buyers, production supervisorsEveryone: finance, sales, operations, HR
AccountingUsually links to a separate package (QuickBooks, Xero, Sage)Built in: general ledger, payables, receivables
Typical cost for a small manufacturerTens to a few hundred dollars per user per monthHigher, plus implementation that often costs more than the first years of licences
ImplementationWeeks to a few monthsSeveral months to a year
ExamplesMRPeasy, Katana, Fishbowl ManufacturingBusiness Central, NetSuite, Odoo, Acumatica, SAP Business One

What each system does day to day

ActivityWith an MRP systemWith an ERP system
Customer orderEntered in MRP; invoice raised in the accounting packageEntered once; flows to production, shipping and invoicing
Material shortageMRP plans purchase orders; receipts update stockSame, and the supplier invoice is matched and paid in the same system
Job costingLabour and material per job in MRP; summary posted to accountingCosts post to the ledger as work is done
Month-endReconcile inventory value between MRP and accountingInventory and ledger are the same data
ReportingProduction reports in MRP; financial reports in accountingCombined reports across sales, production and finance

An MRP system is usually enough when

  • You have fewer than about 25 people using the system and one site.
  • Your accounting package works well and your accountant is happy with it.
  • The main pain is on the shop floor: shortages, late orders, no visibility of work in progress.
  • You need results in weeks, not a year-long project.

It is time to look at ERP when

  • You run several sites, companies or currencies.
  • Finance spends days each month reconciling the MRP system with accounting.
  • You need job or project costing that ties production directly to the ledger.
  • Customers or regulators require full traceability across sales, production and finance.
  • Sales, service and production each keep separate customer and order data.

Five-year cost: a worked comparison

A manufacturer with 30 staff, about 15 of them system users, compares a cloud MRP system linked to its accounting package with a mid-market cloud ERP. All figures are illustrative.

Cost itemMRP + accountingERP
Subscriptions per year$13,200$24,000
Support per yearIncluded$6,000
Implementation partner$12,000$45,000
Data migration and integrationsIncluded above$15,000
TrainingIncluded above$6,000
Internal staff time$15,000$33,000
Five-year total$93,000$249,000

The ERP costs about 2.7 times as much over five years. It is worth it only if it removes costs the MRP route keeps, such as days of month-end reconciliation, double entry between systems, or the risk of errors in inventory valuation, or if it enables growth the MRP route cannot support, such as a second site.

Questions to ask vendors

  • Show us our own order, built from our BOM and routing, from quote to invoice, in a scripted demo.
  • How does the system handle our costing method (standard, average or actual job cost)?
  • What does it integrate with out of the box: our accounting package, e-commerce, shipping, EDI?
  • What is the full five-year cost, including partner fees, support and price increases?
  • Can we export all our data if we leave?
  • Can we speak to two customers of our size and industry?

A common path

Many small manufacturers start with an MRP system linked to their accounting package and move to an ERP as they add sites or complexity. If you take that path, keep your item numbers, BOMs and routings clean from the start; they are what moves to the next system, and clean data shortens any future ERP project by months. Read choosing and implementing an ERP and 10 ERP systems for small and mid-sized businesses when you get there.

Linking MRP to your accounting package

If you choose the MRP route, the link to accounting decides how much manual work remains. Check which of these flow automatically and which need re-keying: customer invoices, supplier bills from purchase orders and receipts, inventory value and cost of goods sold, and labour costs from job time. A good connector posts summaries daily so accounting stays current. Agree with your accountant which system is the master for customers, suppliers and items, and reconcile inventory value monthly; a small difference caught every month is easy to explain, a large one found at year-end is not.

Signs your data is ready for either system

  • Every item has one part number, a description people recognize, a unit of measure and a lead time.
  • BOMs match how products are actually built today.
  • Routings list the real work centres and realistic times.
  • Stock counts match the records for at least 95% of the items you count.
  • Someone owns each of these and keeps them current.

Typical timelines

PhaseMRP systemERP system
Selection2 to 6 weeks2 to 4 months
Data preparation (items, BOMs, routings, stock)2 to 6 weeks1 to 3 months
Configuration and testing2 to 4 weeks2 to 6 months
Training and go-live1 to 2 weeks1 to 2 months

Data preparation is the step most often underestimated, and it is the same work for either system. Doing it well is the best preparation for whichever route you choose.
