10 ERP Systems for Small and Mid-Sized Businesses in 2026, Grouped by Company Size

Quak Foo LeeMar 12, 2023Updated Nov 13, 20265 min read

This list was first published in 2023 with prices that are now out of date. It has been rewritten for 2026 and checked against analyst and review data for company size, so that every system on it is one that small and mid-sized businesses actually buy. For each system it shows the typical company size, the kind of business it fits, how it is priced, and the published price where one exists. Typical sizes are approximate ranges drawn from analyst profiles and review data. It is not a ranking: the right system depends on your processes, your industry and, above all, the implementation partner. For how to choose, see our guide to choosing and implementing an ERP.

These five can be implemented by a small business in a few months with a modest budget. MRPeasy is a manufacturing system first, with lighter accounting, so many users keep their accounting package alongside it. ERPNext costs nothing to license, but implementation is where the money goes, and experienced partners are fewer in Canada than for Odoo or Business Central.

These five suit businesses with more users, more sites or more complex production. They usually need a partner-led project of several months and a budget in the low to mid six figures including implementation. Smaller firms with simple operations will often pay for capability they do not use.

What changed from the 2023 list

  • Two systems were replaced. Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine) is a capable manufacturing ERP, but Infor's mid-market target is 200 to 2,000 employees and even small deployments are estimated at six figures in the first year, so it is replaced by MRPeasy. Sage Intacct is excellent financial software but has no native manufacturing or warehouse management, so it is replaced by ERPNext. Service firms that mainly need strong accounting rather than a full ERP should still look at Sage Intacct.
  • Prices rose. Microsoft raised Business Central list prices on 1 November 2025, its first significant increase in more than five years: Essentials from US$70 to US$80 and Premium from US$100 to US$110 per user per month. Odoo also raises its regional prices periodically.
  • Names changed. Epicor ERP is now sold as Epicor Kinetic, and IQMS, bought by Dassault Systèmes in 2019, is now DELMIAworks.
  • Pricing models diverged. Per-user pricing (Business Central, Odoo, MRPeasy, NetSuite) favours firms with a few heavy users. Acumatica's resource-based model and ERPNext's hosting-only cost favour firms where many people need occasional access.
  • AI features are now standard in sales pitches. Ask to see them working on your own data during the scripted demo, and ask whether they cost extra.

How to narrow the list

  1. Start with your size and growth plan. Choose from the group you will be in three years from now, not just today, to avoid replacing the system again.
  2. Then your industry. Manufacturers need production planning, bills of materials and shop-floor data; distributors need warehouse and pricing depth; project and service firms need project accounting.
  3. Check the local partner. A capable partner in your province with experience in your industry matters more than small feature differences.
  4. Model five years of cost, including your own staff time, for two or three finalists, and run the same scripted demo for each using your own data.

Before a full ERP: all-in-one suites for very small businesses

Below about 20 to 25 people, a full ERP is often premature. A suite of connected business apps, or a good accounting package with an inventory or MRP add-on, may be the better first step.

Zoho One is the leading example. It bundles more than 50 Zoho apps, including Books (accounting), Inventory, CRM, People (HR) and Projects, under one licence: US$37 per employee per month if every employee is licensed, or US$90 per user per month if only some are, both billed annually. Zoho has hosted Canadian customers' data in its own Toronto and Montreal data centres since November 2023, which matters to firms in health care, legal and financial services that must keep data in Canada.

It fits service, retail and light distribution businesses that mainly need accounting, CRM and simple inventory. It is not a manufacturing ERP: Zoho Inventory handles bills of materials and work orders but has no MRP with capacity scheduling and no shop-floor execution. Businesses that grow into several legal entities, heavy inventory or real production usually move to one of the systems above, so plan the data structure with that move in mind.
