After CDAP: Where Canadian Businesses Find Digital and AI Funding in 2026

Updated November 2026. This article first appeared in April 2023, when CDAP grants were open. CDAP has since closed, so it now explains what replaced it. If a website still offers you a $15,000 CDAP grant, that offer is out of date.

For three years the Canada Digital Adoption Program (CDAP) was the easy answer to "who pays for our digital plan?" Its Boost Your Business Technology grant covered most of the cost of a digital adoption plan, up to $15,000, and opened the door to an interest-free BDC loan of up to $100,000 to carry the plan out. Chmltech wrote more than 300 of those plans for Canadian businesses.

The program ended sooner than planned. Boost Your Business Technology was fully subscribed and stopped taking new applications on 19 February 2024. The smaller Grow Your Business Online grant closed as well, and the government scheduled the program to end on 31 March 2025.

No single program took its place. The money for digital adoption and AI is now spread across loans with advice attached, regional repayable contributions, R&D supports and a few provincial plan grants. This article sorts them by what you want to pay for, so you can find your starting point in a few minutes.

Why the landscape looks different now

Two things changed. First, the federal government moved its support for adoption toward larger, repayable instruments. CDAP was a small grant for many firms. Its successors are mostly loans and interest-free contributions for fewer, bigger projects.

Second, AI became the headline. On 4 June 2026 the government released its national AI strategy, AI for All. It aims to raise the share of Canadian businesses using AI from about 12 percent in 2025 to 60 percent by 2034, and it names small and mid-sized firms as a priority. The strategy is a policy package rather than a grant program. In practice, the money reaches businesses through the programs below.

If you need a plan first

This is the gap CDAP left. Outside Ontario and Quebec, no open program pays most of the cost of a general digital plan for a small business. Three routes remain:

  • A provincial plan grant, if you are in Ontario (DMAP) or Quebec (ESSOR component 1B).
  • A plan built into a loan. LIFT's digital and AI path requires a BDC Advisory Services Digital Plan before the loan, and BDC can finance the cost of the consulting.
  • Paying for the plan yourself and treating it as the first, cheapest phase of the project. A good plan usually pays for itself by stopping a poor purchase.

If you are ready to buy and implement

For most small and mid-sized firms the choice is between LIFT and RAII. They are built differently:

RAII repayment typically starts a year after the project ends and runs over five years. Read the terms for your region: the minimum project size, the employee range and the years in operation all differ between agencies.

If you are developing something new

If your project involves real technical uncertainty, such as a new process, a custom model or software that does something standard tools cannot, look at NRC IRAP and SR&ED together. IRAP starts with a conversation with an advisor and can fund part of the project. SR&ED returns part of the qualifying spending as a tax credit after the year ends. Since April 2026 the CRA also offers an elective pre-claim approval, so a business can get the technical eligibility of a project confirmed before it spends the money.

Five lessons CDAP taught that still apply

  1. Apply before you spend. Almost every program reimburses costs incurred after approval, not before. A signed purchase order can disqualify the item.
  2. Plan, then buy. The plan was the most valuable part of CDAP. LIFT and RAII both expect one. Expect to show the process today, the process after, and what changes in hours, errors or revenue.
  3. Measure a baseline. Cycle time, order errors, labour hours, downtime. Without a "before" number you cannot show a benefit, and repayable funding needs a benefit.
  4. Check the stacking limits. Most programs cap total government help on a project, often at 50 to 75 percent of costs. Two approvals can still be cut back if together they exceed the cap.
  5. Budget for repayment. Many of today's programs are loans or repayable contributions. Treat the low or zero interest as a saving on a project worth doing anyway.

Key points

  • CDAP's Boost Your Business Technology grant closed on 19 February 2024, and the program was scheduled to end on 31 March 2025. It is not coming back in its old form.
  • Funding for digital adoption and AI now comes mainly through LIFT, the regional AI initiatives, IRAP, SR&ED, Scale AI and a few provincial programs.
  • Most of the money is repayable, and the projects are larger than CDAP's.
  • Start from what the money will pay for: a plan, a purchase, development work or a joint project.
