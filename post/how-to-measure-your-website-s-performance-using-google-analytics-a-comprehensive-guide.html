How to Measure Your Website with Google Analytics 4: A Small Business Guide

Quak Foo LeeMar 11, 2023Updated Nov 13, 20265 min read

This guide was first written in 2023 for Universal Analytics. Google stopped processing data in standard Universal Analytics properties on 1 July 2023 and has since removed access to them, so every website now measures with Google Analytics 4 (GA4). The ideas are the same: know where visitors come from, what they do, and whether they become enquiries or sales. The tools and names have changed. This rewrite covers what a small business needs to set up and which reports to read each month.

What is different in GA4

  • Everything is an event. A page view, a scroll, a file download and a form submission are all events with details attached.
  • Goals are now key events. You mark the events that matter to the business, such as a form submission or purchase, as key events. Google called these conversions until March 2024.
  • Engagement replaces bounce rate as the main quality measure. GA4 counts a session as engaged if it lasts longer than 10 seconds, includes a key event, or has two or more page views. Bounce rate still exists, but in GA4 it simply means the share of sessions that were not engaged.
  • Detailed data has a time limit. Standard reports keep their aggregated data, but explorations and funnel reports only reach back as far as the data retention setting, which is 2 months by default and 14 months at most on the free version.

Set-up checklist

  1. Set data retention to 14 months (Admin › Data collection and modification › Data retention). The change is not retroactive, so do it on day one.
  2. Mark your key events: contact or quote form submitted, phone number or email clicked, brochure downloaded, purchase. Do not mark page views as key events, or every visit will count as a success.
  3. Filter out your own visits by defining internal traffic for your office IP address.
  4. Link Google Search Console to see which searches bring visitors, and link Google Ads if you advertise.
  5. Handle consent properly. Use a consent banner that matches the privacy laws where your visitors are, and say in your privacy policy that you use analytics.

Six reports to read each month

ReportWhereWhat to look for
Traffic acquisitionReports › AcquisitionSessions, engagement rate and key events by channel
Pages and screensReports › EngagementWhich pages people read, and which lead to key events
Landing pageReports › EngagementWhich entry pages engage visitors and which lose them
Key eventsReports › EngagementCount of each key event, month on month
Search Console queriesReports › Search Console (once linked)Searches you appear for, clicks and average position
Tech: device and browserReports › TechWhether mobile visitors engage less, a sign of a layout problem

Worked example: one month on a B2B website

An engineering services firm has a website with a quote request form, marked as its key event. One month's numbers by channel are below; they are illustrative.

ChannelSessionsEngagement rateQuote requestsKey event rate

Referral (incl. AI assistants)18067%31.67%

Organic search brings the most sessions and the most quote requests. Referral traffic, which now includes visits from AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Perplexity, is small but converts at 1.7%, the highest rate of any channel, which suggests that people arriving from an AI answer have already done their research. Organic social brings visitors who rarely engage. Three actions follow: improve the pages that rank in search but have low engagement, make sure the site is easy for AI assistants to cite (see SEO vs GEO), and spend less effort chasing social traffic that does not convert.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving data retention at 2 months and losing a year of detail.
  • Counting your own team's visits.
  • Marking too many or the wrong events as key events.
  • Comparing GA4 bounce rates with old Universal Analytics figures; the definitions differ.
  • Reading traffic without key events. More visitors is only good if more of them call, write or buy.
