SR&ED for Engineering and Software Projects: What Changed in 2026

Quak Foo LeeNov 13, 20269 min read

Many engineering firms and plants do SR&ED-eligible work every year and never claim it. They assume the program is for laboratories and software start-ups. It is not. A plant that cannot get a new formulation to scale, or an engineering team that writes software to control something no standard package can handle, may well qualify.

2026 made the program more valuable. The enhanced credit limit doubled, equipment became eligible again, and the CRA started confirming projects before the work begins. Budget 2026 proposes to widen it further. This article explains what qualifies in engineering and software, what changed, and how to estimate a claim.

What changed in 2026

Certainty before you startPre-claim consultation, no binding answerPre-claim approval, from 1 April 2026
Regulatory testing and IP costsNot eligibleProposed in Budget 2026, for tax years starting on or after 5 November 2026

The first four changes were enacted by Bill C-15, which received Royal Assent on 26 March 2026. The Budget 2026 changes are proposals.

What qualifies: the five questions

SR&ED is defined by the work, not the industry. The CRA asks five questions about each project.

The first question decides most claims. If a competent engineer could solve the problem with standard practice, published methods or known correlations, there is no technological uncertainty, however hard the work was. Difficulty is not uncertainty.

Engineering and process work: examples

Likely SR&EDNot SR&ED
Finding why a reaction that works at lab scale gives poor yield in the pilot plant, when published data do not explain itSizing a reactor, pump or heat exchanger with standard methods
Developing a new catalyst, membrane or separation step and testing variants systematicallySelecting equipment from vendor catalogues
Building a model to predict a property no correlation covers, and validating it against plant dataA HAZOP, a design review or a routine debottleneck
Trials to make a product meet a specification that existing processes cannot reachRoutine quality control testing and commissioning

Software work: examples

Software projects qualify when the uncertainty is technological: how to make the system do something, not what it should do.

  • Likely SR&ED: a control algorithm that has to run within a time limit standard libraries cannot meet; a machine learning model where no known method reaches the required accuracy on your data, and you test alternatives systematically; integrating systems where the published interfaces cannot deliver the required performance and a new approach must be found.
  • Not SR&ED: configuring an ERP or CRM, building a website or app with standard frameworks, connecting systems through their documented APIs, data migration, user interface design and routine bug fixing.

A useful test: if your team could describe the solution on day one and the rest was effort, it is probably not SR&ED. If they had to try things to find out whether it could be done, it may be.

Estimating a claim

Take a Canadian-controlled private corporation with a project that meets the five questions. In one year it spends:

ItemSpentRuleQualifying
Salaries of staff doing SR&ED$600,000Time spent on SR&ED$600,000
Overhead (proxy method)–55% of the salary base$330,000
Materials consumed in tests$50,000Consumed or transformed$50,000
Contract testing by an arm's-length lab$200,00080% of contract payments$160,000
Test rig (capital)$100,000Eligible again since December 2024$100,000
Total$1,240,000

At the enhanced 35 percent rate, the federal credit is about $434,000. Because the company is under the $6 million limit, the credit on current spending is refundable: it is paid out even if the company owes no tax. In British Columbia a further 10 percent provincial credit applies to SR&ED done in the province. Provincial credits reduce the federal base, so the combined figure is a little less than the sum of the two. This is a simplified example. Salaries of owners and specified employees, government funding received and many other rules change the result.

Pre-claim approval: worth considering

Since 1 April 2026, a business can ask the CRA to confirm that planned work is SR&ED before it starts the work or spends the money. The CRA describes the process this way:

  1. Request a case number, then apply in My Business Account for up to three projects.
  2. Meet a CRA SR&ED specialist within about four weeks.
  3. Receive a decision within about eight weeks of completing the application. An approval lasts up to three years.

Claims for approved projects that need an expenditure review are processed in 90 days instead of 180. The approval covers technical eligibility only. The CRA can still review the costs. To apply, the business must be in good standing, and the work must not have been claimed already.

Pre-claim approval suits a first-time claimant, a large project, or a project whose eligibility is a judgement call. It needs a clear written description of the uncertainty before work starts, which is good discipline in any case.

Records that make a claim stand up

  • A short project plan at the start: the uncertainty, the hypothesis and the planned tests.
  • Dated test logs, run sheets, lab notebooks, code commits and analysis files.
  • Time records that tie each person's hours to the project.
  • Invoices for materials and contracts, marked to the project.
  • A note at the end: what was learned, including what failed.

Claims are filed with the tax return on Form T661 and the related schedules. The general deadline is 18 months after the end of the tax year. Late claims are not accepted.

Key points

  • SR&ED is about technological uncertainty, not industry. Engineering, process and software work can all qualify.
  • In 2026 the enhanced credit limit doubled to $6 million, equipment became eligible again, and the CRA began approving projects before they start.
  • Budget 2026 proposes to add first regulatory approvals and IP protection costs.
  • Keep records as you go. A strong claim is built during the project, not after it.
