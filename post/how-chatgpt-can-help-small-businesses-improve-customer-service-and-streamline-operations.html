AI in Customer Service for Small Businesses: Uses, Guardrails and a Worked Example

Quak Foo LeeMar 11, 2023Updated Nov 13, 20264 min read

AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and the assistants built into help desk software can now handle much of the routine work in customer service: drafting replies, sorting incoming messages, summarizing long threads and answering common questions. Used well, they make a small team faster and more consistent. Used carelessly, they give customers wrong answers in your company's name. This article, first written in 2023, has been rewritten with practical uses, a Canadian legal lesson and a worked example.

Where AI helps, from lowest to highest risk

UseHow it worksRisk
Draft repliesAI drafts an answer from your policies and order data; a person checks and sendsLow
Triage and routingClassify incoming emails and tickets by topic and urgency; send to the right personLow
SummariesSummarize long threads or calls so the next person picks up quicklyLow
Knowledge baseTurn resolved tickets into help articles; keep them currentLow to medium
Website chat for routine questionsAnswer hours, order status, returns policy from approved contentMedium
Actions on the customer's behalfChange orders, issue refunds, book appointmentsHigh: needs strict limits and review

Start at the top of the table. Uses where a person reviews the output before a customer sees it deliver most of the time savings with little risk.

A Canadian lesson: you are responsible for what your chatbot says

In Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024), British Columbia's Civil Resolution Tribunal found Air Canada liable after its website chatbot gave a customer wrong information about bereavement fares. The airline argued, in effect, that the chatbot was responsible for its own statements; the tribunal rejected that and held the company responsible for all information on its website, whether from a static page or a chatbot. The amount awarded was small, but the principle matters for every business: an AI assistant speaking to customers is speaking for you.

Guardrails that keep AI answers safe

  1. Ground answers in your own content. Connect the assistant to approved policies, product information and order data, and instruct it to answer only from them.
  2. Tell it what to do when unsure: say so and hand over to a person, rather than guess.
  3. Keep people in the loop for refunds, exceptions, complaints and anything involving money, safety or legal terms.
  4. Make handover easy. Customers should be able to reach a person without fighting the bot.
  5. Protect personal information. Use business plans of AI tools that do not train on your data, and follow your privacy obligations.
  6. Review a sample of conversations weekly, and fix the content or instructions behind any wrong answer.

Worked example: email support at a distributor

A parts distributor's two-person service team answers about 60 customer emails a day, at about 6 minutes each. They add an assistant inside their help desk that classifies each email, pulls the order details and drafts a reply from the company's policies; staff edit and send. Average handling time falls to about 3.5 minutes. That frees roughly 625 hours a year, worth about $20,000 at $32 an hour, which the team uses to call back customers with complex problems and to clear the backlog of quote requests. Replies also become more consistent, because they all start from the same policy text. The figures are illustrative.

MeasureWhat it shows

Customer satisfaction (CSAT)Whether faster replies are also good replies
Escalation rateHow often AI or staff hand over to a specialist

Choosing tools

There are two routes. Help desk platforms such as Zendesk, Freshdesk, HubSpot Service Hub or Zoho Desk now include AI drafting, triage and chat that work on your tickets and knowledge base directly; they are the easiest route for a team that already uses one. General assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft Copilot are flexible for drafting and summarizing but need careful handling of customer data and do not see your order system unless connected. For most small teams, AI inside the help desk or CRM you already use is the right first step.

How to start

  1. Collect your top 30 questions and the correct answers; this becomes the assistant's source material and a help page.
  2. Pick one channel, usually email, and use AI for drafts and triage only.
  3. Measure first response time, handling time, resolution rate and customer satisfaction before and after.
  4. Expand to website chat for routine questions once draft quality is consistently good.
