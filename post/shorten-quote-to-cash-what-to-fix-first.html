Shorten Quote to Cash: What to Fix First and the Payback

Quak Foo LeeNov 14, 20264 min read

Part 1 showed that an order at Example Windows Co. takes 66 days to become cash and that about $1.06 million sits in open orders. Part 2 gave you the model. This post uses it to decide what to fix first.

Six fixes, tested one at a time

Each fix was run in the simulator on its own for one simulated year. Everything else stayed at the starting settings.

Table 1. Effect of each fix on its own. Days are quote-to-cash days.

FixChangeDaysCash tied upCash freedOn time
Starting point66.3$1,061,00080%
Invoice on delivery dayInvoice lag 5 → 0 days61.1$966,000$96,00080%
Follow up overdue invoicesLate payment 10 → 3 days59.5$938,000$123,00080%
Take a 30% depositDeposit 0 → 30%66.3$497,000$564,00080%
Quote in one dayQuote turnaround 5 → 1 day62.1$1,062,000None83%
Halve the batch sizeBatch 4 → 2 orders65.3$1,049,000$12,00097%
Raise machine uptimeUptime 90 → 95%65.7$1,053,000$8,00090%

What the results say

  • Three fixes free cash, and all three are in the office. The deposit frees the most, about $564,000. Following up overdue invoices frees about $123,000 and invoicing on delivery day about $96,000.
  • A faster quote saves days and frees no cash. Nothing has been spent on an order while it is being quoted. The value of a one-day quote is commercial. The model does not count orders won or lost, so it cannot show that gain.
  • The factory fixes protect the delivery promise. Smaller batches lift on-time delivery from 80% to 97%, and better uptime lifts it to 90%. Neither moves much cash.

On-time figures within about 3 points of each other are the same result, because the simulation is random.

Rank the fixes by effort

Start with the fixes that cost the least and need no agreement from customers.

Table 2. Suggested order of work

OrderFixEffortWhat it takesOwner
1Invoice on delivery dayLowRaise the invoice from the delivery confirmation. Capture proof of delivery on a phone.Finance
2Follow up overdue invoicesLowAutomatic reminders before and after the due date. One person owns the overdue list.Finance
3Take a depositMediumNew terms on quotes, applied to new orders first. Sales needs to explain the change.Sales and the owner

The office package

Fixes 1 to 4 together take quote to cash from 66 days to 50. Cash tied up falls from $1.06 million to about $386,000, which frees about $675,000. Adding the two factory fixes brings the total to 49 days, and every order in the simulated year arrives on time.

A deposit is the largest item and the hardest to win. Some customers will refuse it. Even a 10% deposit on new orders frees a useful share of the amount, and the simulator in Part 2 lets you test any level.

The payback

Freed cash is a one-time release, not profit. Its yearly value is the cost of that money: the interest on a credit line, or what the cash could earn elsewhere in the business.

Try it: payback on the cash you free

The starting values are the office package in this example, with an assumed 7% cost of borrowing and an assumed project cost of $60,000.

In the example, $675,000 at 7% is worth about $47,250 a year. A $60,000 project covering the invoicing change, reminders, a price book and a configurator pays back in about 15 months on interest alone. The cash released is about 11 times the project cost. Both the 7% and the $60,000 are assumptions. Use your own figures in the calculator.

A 90-day plan

Table 3. What to do in the first 90 days

DaysWorkResult to check
1 to 30Record six dates for every order: request, quote, order, delivery, invoice and payment. Invoice on delivery day. Turn on payment reminders.Invoice lag under 1 day
31 to 60Put deposit terms on new quotes. Build the price book for the most common products.Share of new orders with a deposit. Quote turnaround.
61 to 90Try a smaller batch on one product family. Log every machine stoppage.On-time delivery. Days sales outstanding.

What the model leaves out

  • Orders won or lost. A faster quote and a deposit both change how many customers say yes.
  • Bad debts. Every invoice in the model is paid in the end.
  • Weekends and holidays. The model runs every day.
  • Your own mix. The example has one product at one price. Real orders vary in size, margin and terms.

Takeaway. Fix the office first. In the example, four changes that need no new equipment cut quote to cash from 66 days to 50 and free about $675,000. Then use smaller batches and better uptime to protect the delivery promise.
