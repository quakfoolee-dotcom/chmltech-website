Overview

How do you improve care when budgets are tight?

Hospitals, clinics, long-term care homes and community health providers face rising demand, staffing pressure and fixed budgets. We know that improvement takes more than a boardroom conversation. Chmltech collaborates with your staff, physicians and union partners in relatable ways to improve patient flow, reduce wait times and remove work that adds no value for patients. We develop process measures that communicate your value from frontline to executive and act as your coaches in performance improvement. The result is a culture of continuous improvement that keeps delivering after the project ends.

Signs it's time to act

  • Wait times for appointments, procedures or beds keep growing
  • Patients are discharged late in the day, blocking new admissions
  • Staff spend too much time searching for supplies or information
  • Overtime and agency staffing costs are hard to control
  • Managers lack timely, trusted data on patient flow

Industry challenges

What we see in health care.

Patient flow and wait times

Delays in one area, such as discharge or diagnostics, ripple through the system and lengthen waits for patients.

Staffing and workload

Clinical and support staff are stretched, so wasted steps and rework directly affect morale and patient experience.

Cost control

Organizations must fulfil their duty to the communities they serve while reducing capital and operating expenses.

Engaging staff and unions

Change imposed from the top meets resistance. Lasting improvement needs staff and union partners involved from the start.

How we help

How we help

KPIs and performance measures

We define metrics for service levels, productivity, speed and quality, such as wait times and length of stay, and set a baseline to measure success.

Patient flow improvement

We map the patient journey from referral or arrival to discharge, find the delays and work with teams to reduce wait times.

Current-state review

A thorough as-is analysis: process mapping, financial and operating data review, direct observation and a review of current management tools.

Non-value-added activity analysis

Observations of clinical and support work separate what adds value for patients from waste such as searching, waiting and duplicate documentation.

Change management with staff and unions

We assess change readiness and build a plan that involves staff and union partners, so your people drive and own the change.

Management tools and digital workflows

We help leadership turn strategy into daily activities, using visual boards, huddles and practical automation of scheduling and reporting.

Typical scenarios

What improvement looks like.

Illustrative examples of the kind of change we work on.

Discharge planning on a medical unit

Before: Most discharges happened late in the afternoon, leaving emergency patients waiting for beds.

After: Expected discharge dates and a morning huddle move more discharges earlier in the day.

Supply rooms in a community clinic

Before: Nurses walked between rooms looking for supplies that were often out of stock.

After: Standardized supply locations and a simple reorder system keep items where and when staff need them.

Outpatient appointment booking

Before: Referrals were tracked on paper and phone calls, and patients were unsure when they would be seen.

After: A single digital referral queue gives staff a clear view of waits and lets them book patients sooner.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do you work with unionized health care environments?

Yes. We involve union partners early and work collaboratively with staff, which builds trust and helps changes last.

Will this add work for already busy clinical staff?

We design sessions to fit around patient care and shift patterns. The aim is to remove wasted steps so staff have more time for patients, not less.

What kinds of health care organizations do you support?

We work with hospitals, clinics, long-term care homes, community health providers and health-related service organizations across Canada.

How do you measure improvement in health care?

We agree KPIs such as wait times, length of stay, throughput and staff time on direct care, set a baseline and track results against it and an agreed ROI target.
